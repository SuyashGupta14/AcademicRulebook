### Section 1: Title, Jurisdiction, and Commencement

1.1. These ordinances and regulations shall be designated as the **"SGSITS Autonomous Academic Regulations and Student Governance Code (2024 Revision)"**.  
1.2. These regulations apply comprehensively to all undergraduate (Bachelor of Technology — B.Tech., Bachelor of Pharmacy — B.Pharm.), postgraduate (Master of Technology — M.Tech., Master of Pharmacy — M.Pharm., Master of Business Administration — MBA, Master of Computer Applications — MCA), and Doctoral (Ph.D.) programs offered in regular or self-financing modes across all academic departments of the Institute.  
1.3. These regulations shall come into full statutory force on the 1st of July, 2024, superseding all prior ordinances, circulars, notifications, and departmental memoranda concerning student attendance, examination eligibility, academic assessment, hostel tenancy, disciplinary procedures, and fee schedules.  
1.4. The jurisdiction for all legal disputes, claims, or constitutional challenges arising out of the administration, interpretation, or enforcement of these regulations shall lie exclusively within the territorial jurisdiction of the competent courts of law situated in Indore, Madhya Pradesh, India.  
1.5. In the event of any irreconcilable ambiguity between the English-language version and any Hindi or regional language translation of this text, the authorized English version countersigned by the Director shall remain the sole legal authority.

### Section 2: Comprehensive Definitions

Unless repugnant to the subject or contextual meaning, the terms defined in this section shall carry the assigned interpretations throughout this code:
- **"Institute"** signifies the Shri Govindram Seksaria Institute of Technology and Science (SGSITS), located at 23 Sir M. Visvesvaraya Marg, Vallabh Nagar, Indore, Madhya Pradesh.
- **"Director"** designates the executive head and principal administrative officer of the Institute appointed by the Board of Governors.
- **"Dean of Academic Affairs"** (Dean Academics) signifies the senior faculty member designated to oversee curricula, registration, grading standards, academic calendar adherence, and ordinance enforcement.
- **"Dean of Student Welfare"** (DSW) designates the administrative authority responsible for co-curricular affairs, student grievances, scholarship oversight, and non-academic campus welfare.
- **"Head of Department"** (HOD) designates the statutory head of a teaching or research department of the Institute.
- **"Controller of Examinations"** (COE) designates the officer charged with the administration, printing, custody, evaluation, tabulation, and declaration of all mid-semester and end-semester examinations.
- **"Course"** denotes a distinct instructional unit consisting of lectures (L), tutorials (T), and/or practical sessions (P), identified by a unique alphanumeric course code and credit weightage.
- **"Semester"** refers to an academic duration typically comprising 90 instructional days (excluding examination days and recess periods) structured as either an Autumn Semester (Odd) or Spring Semester (Even).
- **"Credit"** signifies a quantitative metric: 1 credit corresponds to 1 hour of lecture per week, 1 hour of tutorial per week, or 2 hours of laboratory/practical work per week across a 15-week instructional semester.
- **"Continuous Internal Evaluation"** (CIE) denotes cumulative internal formative assessment comprising class tests, quizzes, laboratory assessments, seminars, and assignments.
- **"End-Semester Examination"** (ESE) represents the terminal summative written or practical examination administered under the supervision of the Controller of Examinations.
- **"SGPA"** denotes Semester Grade Point Average, calculated as the weighted average of grade points earned in all courses enrolled during that semester.
- **"CGPA"** denotes Cumulative Grade Point Average, computed as the cumulative weighted average of grade points across all registered semesters.

### Section 3: Academic Admission, Course Registration, and Enrollment Continuity

3.1. Admission to all undergraduate degree programs is conducted strictly through the Directorate of Technical Education (DTE), Government of Madhya Pradesh, via centralized counselling founded on Joint Entrance Examination (JEE Main) ranks.  
3.2. Every candidate admitted must submit verified original documentation including high school mark sheets, domicile certificates, caste verification credentials (if applicable), and medical fitness declarations within 14 calendar days of counselling allotment.  
3.3. **Pre-Semester Course Registration**:  
(a) Every student must formally register for the prescribed schedule of courses on the ERP portal prior to the commencement of instructional classes for each semester.  
(b) Late course registration is permissible up to 7 calendar days after classes commence with payment of a ₹1,000 late registration levy, provided written endorsement is obtained from the respective Head of Department.  
(c) No student shall be allowed to register for any course after the expiration of 10 calendar days from the official beginning of semester classes under any circumstances.  
3.4. **Credit Ceilings and Registration Limits**:  
(a) The standard semester curriculum prescribes an academic credit load between 20 and 26 credits per semester.  
(b) A student carrying academic backlogs may register for backlog courses concurrently, subject to an absolute maximum ceiling of 30 total registered credits in a single semester.  
(c) Students maintaining a CGPA below 4.50 at the conclusion of an academic year are placed on Academic Monitoring and are restricted to a maximum load of 18 credits in the subsequent term.

### Section 4: Mandatory Attendance, Monitoring, and Condonation Protocols

4.1. **The 75% Mandatory Attendance Rule**:  
Every registered candidate is statutorily required to attend a minimum of **75%** of all scheduled lectures, tutorials, practical laboratory classes, and assigned departmental workshops in each enrolled subject individually to establish eligibility for appearing in the End-Semester Examination (ESE).  
4.2. **Calculation of Subject-Wise Attendance**:  
Attendance percentage is computed strictly on a subject-by-subject basis using the mathematical formula:  
`Attendance (%) = (Total instructional classes attended by student / Total instructional classes conducted by faculty) * 100`  
Attendance across separate subjects cannot be aggregated, combined, or averaged to compensate for an attendance deficiency in any individual course.  
4.3. **Institutional Condonation for Medical or Extraordinary Circumstances**:  
(a) The Director of the Institute is vested with sole statutory discretion to condone an attendance deficiency of up to, but not exceeding, **10%** (thereby establishing an absolute minimum attendance threshold of **65%**) strictly on the grounds of acute hospitalization, severe incapacitating illness, or authorized representation of the Institute in official national-level athletic, academic, or cultural events.  
(b) To seek medical condonation, the student or their authorized guardian must file a formal application accompanied by genuine inpatient hospitalization records, discharge summaries, and diagnostics authenticated by a Registered Medical Practitioner (holding at least an M.B.B.S. credential) with the respective Head of Department within seven (7) working days of resumption of campus attendance.  
(c) Medical applications tendered post the declaration of the provisional detention list or at the conclusion of the teaching term shall be summarily rejected without inquiry.  
4.4. **Detention from Examination (The 'Z' Grade)**:  
(a) Any candidate whose final verified subject attendance falls below 75% (or below 65% in cases of sanctioned Director's condonation) shall be officially classified as **Detained** in that course.  
(b) The Controller of Examinations shall debar the detained student from sitting for the End-Semester Examination in that subject, and a grade of **'Z'** (Detained due to attendance shortage) shall be recorded on the official tabulation register.  
(c) A student awarded a 'Z' grade must re-enroll, re-attend all lectures and laboratory sessions, and re-pay the subject registration fees when the course is offered in a subsequent regular semester.  
4.5. **Weekly Monitoring and ERP Publication**:  
All faculty course coordinators must upload verified weekly attendance figures to the Institute ERP system by 5:00 PM every Saturday. Cumulative subject-wise attendance rosters shall be published on departmental notice boards on the final working day of each calendar month.

### Section 5: Examination Architecture, Grading System, and Degree Classification

5.1. **Components of Assessment**:  
The academic evaluation for every theoretical credit course comprises two distinct components:  
(a) Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) carrying 40% weightage, comprising two mandatory Mid-Semester Tests (MST-1 and MST-2, 20 marks each, averaged or aggregated as per scheme), quizzes/assignments (10 marks), and classroom engagement/attendance (10 marks).  
(b) End-Semester Examination (ESE) carrying 60% weightage, administered in a centralized 3-hour closed-book format.  
5.2. **Minimum Pass Thresholds**:  
(a) To secure a passing credit in any theoretical course, a candidate must obtain at least 35% marks in Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) AND at least **40%** marks in the End-Semester Examination (ESE) independently, with an aggregate score of not less than 40% across both components.  
(b) In practical laboratory courses, a student must secure a minimum of 50% in continuous laboratory assessment and 50% in the terminal viva-voce/practical examination.  
5.3. **Standard 10-Point Absolute Grading Scale**:  
Performance in each course is quantified through letter grades carrying specified grade points:  
- **Grade 'O'** (Outstanding): 90% to 100% marks | Grade Point = 10.0  
- **Grade 'A+'** (Excellent): 80% to 89% marks | Grade Point = 9.0  
- **Grade 'A'** (Very Good): 70% to 79% marks | Grade Point = 8.0  
- **Grade 'B+'** (Good): 60% to 69% marks | Grade Point = 7.0  
- **Grade 'B'** (Above Average): 50% to 59% marks | Grade Point = 6.0  
- **Grade 'C'** (Average): 45% to 49% marks | Grade Point = 5.0  
- **Grade 'P'** (Pass): 40% to 44% marks | Grade Point = 4.0  
- **Grade 'F'** (Fail): Below 40% marks | Grade Point = 0.0  
- **Grade 'I'** (Incomplete): Temporary grade due to sanctioned medical absence during examination | Grade Point = 0.0  
- **Grade 'Z'** (Detained): Debarred from examination due to attendance shortage (<75%) | Grade Point = 0.0  
5.4. **Computation of SGPA and CGPA**:  
`SGPA = Σ (C_i * G_i) / Σ C_i`, where `C_i` is the credit of course `i` and `G_i` is the grade point earned.  
`CGPA = Σ (C_k * G_k) / Σ C_k`, calculated over all completed academic semesters.  
5.5. **Dean's Merit List & Scholarship Entitlement Clause**:  
Students who maintain exemplary academic performance and achieve a **CGPA of 7.50 or higher** without any concurrent or historical backlogs at the close of an academic year shall be formally named to the **Dean's Merit Honor Roll**. Students holding Dean's Merit standing shall be entitled to **automatic annual renewal of all institutional merit scholarships and fee waivers** without being subjected to external review committees or separate application cycles.  
5.6. **Graduation and Degree Division Classification**:  
- First Division with Honors: CGPA of 8.50 and above, completed within the stipulated 4-year duration without any backlog history.  
- First Division: CGPA of 6.50 to 8.49.  
- Second Division: CGPA of 5.00 to 6.49.  
- Minimum qualifying threshold for award of Bachelor of Technology degree is a cumulative CGPA of 5.00.

### Section 6: Examination Conduct, Malpractice Penalties, and Academic Integrity

6.1. **Admit Cards and Verification**:  
No candidate shall be permitted entrance into any examination hall without presenting an official Examination Admit Card verified by the Controller of Examinations and a valid Institute Student Identity Card.  
6.2. **Prohibited Materials in Examination Halls**:  
(a) Possession of mobile cellular phones, smartwatches, programmable calculators, electronic storage peripherals, unauthorized scribbled papers, books, notes, or verbal/visual communication with peers is strictly forbidden.  
(b) Possession of an unauthorized electronic communication device, regardless of whether operational or powered off, shall automatically constitute prima facie evidence of an intent to commit academic malpractice.  
6.3. **Malpractice Classification and Punitive Schedules**:  
- **Category 1 (Minor Malpractice)**: Copying from scribbled notes or adjacent candidate without resistance — Cancellation of the specific subject paper; candidate awarded 'F' grade in the examination paper.  
- **Category 2 (Moderate Malpractice)**: Possession and usage of electronic devices, programmable media, or exchange of answer scripts — Cancellation of the entire current semester's examinations across all enrolled subjects; student awarded 'F' grade across all semester registrations.  
- **Category 3 (Severe Malpractice / Impersonation)**: Impersonation of another examinee, assault on invigilation staff, or unauthorized removal of answer scripts — Immediate expulsion from the Institute for a minimum duration of four (4) consecutive semesters or permanent rustication with formal intimation to law enforcement authorities.  
6.4. **Plagiarism Policy and Turnitin Benchmarks**:  
(a) In alignment with UGC (Promotion of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism in Higher Educational Institutions) Regulations 2018, all B.Tech. major project dissertations, M.Tech. theses, and doctoral dissertations must undergo similarity indexing through Institute-licensed Turnitin software prior to formal submission.  
(b) The maximum permissible similarity index threshold is strictly **15%**, excluding references, bibliographies, mathematical formulae, and standardized institutional declarations.  
(c) **Plagiarism Penalties**:  
- Level 0 (Similarity up to 10%): Approved without penalty.  
- Level 1 (Similarity 11% to 15%): Accepted with minor revisions within 7 days.  
- Level 2 (Similarity 16% to 40%): The candidate shall be denied submission and instructed to revise and re-submit the thesis after a mandatory incubation interval of six (6) months.  
- Level 3 (Similarity above 40%): Cancellation of registration and debarment from program submission for one full academic year.

### Section 7: Academic Backlogs, Re-Appearances, and Year Detention

7.1. **Yearly Promotion Rules (ATKT — Allowed To Keep Term)**:  
(a) A student is permitted promotion from the 1st Year (Semesters I and II) to the 2nd Year (Semester III) provided they do not carry backlogs in more than four (4) theoretical courses across both semesters combined.  
(b) Promotion from the 2nd Year (Semesters III and IV) to the 3rd Year (Semester V) requires that the student must have completely cleared all courses of the 1st Year with zero remaining backlogs.  
(c) Promotion from the 3rd Year to the 4th Year requires total clearance of all 2nd Year courses.  
7.2. **Supplemental and Remedial Examinations**:  
Students failing in any End-Semester Examination shall have the opportunity to appear for Remedial Backlog Examinations conducted during the inter-semester vacation period upon payment of ₹600 per registered paper.  
7.3. **Maximum Time Limit for Degree Completion**:  
The maximum permissible tenure for completing the requirements of a 4-year B.Tech. degree program is seven (7) academic years from the date of initial matriculation. Candidates unable to fulfill degree requirements within seven years shall forfeit their academic enrollment.

### Section 8: Tuition Schedules, Late Fees, and Financial Governance

8.1. All registered students are required to remit all prescribed semester tuition fees, developmental levies, laboratory charges, and examination fees in accordance with the schedules notified by the Finance and Accounts Division at the inception of each term.  
8.2. Fees must be remitted exclusively via the authenticated online State Bank of India Collect (SBI Collect) or Institute ERP gateway. Cash payments, physical drafts, or private UPI transfers are strictly prohibited.  
8.3. **Standard Fee Deadlines and Penalties**:  
The regular fee remittance window without penalty closes 21 calendar days prior to the first day of instructional classes. A standard late fee of ₹100 per working day is charged thereafter up to the final cutoff date published in the Institutional Fee Schedule.  
8.4. **Committee Authority on Late Fee Window Extension**:  
Notwithstanding the standard published fee payment schedules and late penalty cutoff windows, the **Academic Committee of the Institute**, acting upon formal recommendation of the Dean of Academic Affairs, is empowered with full administrative discretion to extend the final permissible date for semester fee payment with late fee up to **15th November** for the Autumn Semester upon consideration of demonstrated hardship, banking transit delays, or government scholarship distribution bottlenecks.  
8.5. **Fee Default Consequences**:  
Failure to remit all due institutional fees prior to the expiry of the sanctioned window shall result in the immediate deactivation of the student's ERP credentials, withholding of examination admit cards, and temporary suspension of hostel residency privileges.

### Section 9: Hostel Residence, Tenancy Rules, and Code of Living

9.1. **Hostel Allocation and Eligibility**:  
Residency in Institute hostels is a privilege and cannot be claimed as a matter of statutory right. Preference is accorded to outstation candidates on merit based on JEE Main ranks for first-year entrants and preceding annual CGPA for senior students.  
9.2. **Fee Architecture for Hostellers**:  
(a) Every allotted hostel resident must pay an institutional Room Rent and Maintenance Fee of **₹31,450 per semester**.  
(b) Every resident must remit an upfront advance Mess Fee of **₹16,500 per semester** managed on a cooperative, non-profit operational model by the Warden Council.  
(c) A one-time refundable hostel caution deposit of ₹5,000 is payable upon initial hostel admission.  
9.3. **Curfew and Gate Timings**:  
(a) For security and discipline, all residents in the Boys' Hostels (Hostels 1 through 6) must return to the hostel premises by **10:00 PM** on all weekdays and weekends.  
(b) All residents in the Girls' Hostels (Sarojini Naidu, Kasturba, and Rani Laxmibai Bhavans) must return to hostel premises by **9:00 PM** on all days.  
(c) Late entry beyond prescribed curfew timings requires prior recorded written authorization from the Chief Warden or resident hostel warden. Three unauthorized late entries in a semester shall lead to automatic eviction from the hostel.  
9.4. **Prohibited Items and Behavioral Standards**:  
(a) The possession, consumption, or facilitation of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, tobacco products, cigarettes, e-cigarettes, or intoxicating substances inside hostel rooms or common premises is strictly prohibited and attracts immediate rustication and police referral.  
(b) High-power electrical heating appliances (immersion rods, electric heaters, induction stoves) are strictly prohibited in hostel rooms. Violation attracts a fine of ₹2,500 and seizure of the appliance.  
(c) Day scholars and unauthorized external guests are forbidden from staying overnight in hostel rooms. Permitting an unauthorized guest to lodge overnight incurs an immediate penalty of ₹5,000 on the host resident.

### Section 10: Anti-Ragging Enactments and Student Discipline

10.1. **Zero Tolerance Against Ragging**:  
In compliance with the directives of the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India and AICTE/UGC Regulations, the Institute maintains an absolute **Zero Tolerance Policy** toward ragging in any form, direct or indirect, verbal, physical, psychological, or electronic.  
10.2. **Definition of Ragging Actions**:  
Ragging includes any disorderly conduct, tease, abuse, handling with rudeness, asking students to perform humiliating acts, or causing physical or psychological harm to any student.  
10.3. **Mandatory Penalties for Proven Ragging**:  
Upon recommendation of the Institute Anti-Ragging Committee and Anti-Ragging Squad, the Director shall impose one or more of the following sanctions against any student found guilty:  
(a) Suspension from attending academic classes and academic privileges.  
(b) Withholding or withdrawing scholarship, fellowship, and other financial benefits.  
(c) Debarring from appearing in any test, examination, or evaluation process.  
(d) Withholding examination results.  
(e) Suspension or expulsion from the hostel mess and residential accommodations.  
(f) Cancellation of institutional admission.  
(g) Rustication from the Institute for a period ranging from one to four semesters.  
(h) Expulsion from the Institute and consequent debarment from admission to any other technical institution for a specified period.  
(i) Lodging of a First Information Report (FIR) with the Tukoganj / MG Road Police Station under relevant sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) / Indian Penal Code.

### Section 11: Academic Grievance Redressal, Re-Evaluation, and Appellate Powers

11.1. **Right to Inspect Answer Scripts**:  
A student dissatisfied with their theoretical End-Semester Examination evaluation may apply for physical inspection of their evaluated answer script within seven (7) working days of the official declaration of provisional results, upon payment of a scrutiny fee of ₹300 per paper.  
11.2. **Challenge Re-Evaluation Process**:  
(a) If an examinee detects substantive discrepancies during script inspection, they may file for formal Challenge Re-Evaluation within ten (10) working days of inspection, upon payment of ₹1,500 per subject.  
(b) The answer script shall be masked, sanitized, and remitted to an independent external evaluator drawn from an approved panel of university professors.  
(c) If the revised mark differs from the original score by 10% or more of the total paper marks, the revised score shall supersede the original score, and 50% of the challenge fee shall be refunded to the candidate.  
11.3. **The Standing Grievance Committee**:  
All student academic grievances that cannot be resolved at the departmental level shall be adjudicated by the Standing Academic Appeals Committee chaired by the Dean of Academic Affairs, with determinations submitted to the Director for final executive sanction.

### Section 12: Central Library Governance, Digital Repositories, and Book Bank Scheme

12.1. **Registration and Access Protocols**:  
All registered undergraduate, postgraduate, and research scholars are automatically enrolled as primary members of the Central Library upon generation of their permanent Institute enrollment number and issuance of the biometric RFID Smart Identity Card. Access to reading halls, stack areas, reference sections, and the digital knowledge commons is strictly contingent upon presenting the active RFID credential at automated turnstiles.  
12.2. **Circulation Entitlements and Borrowing Periods**:  
(a) Undergraduate B.Tech. and B.Pharm. students are entitled to borrow up to four (4) general collection volumes simultaneously for an active lending duration of fourteen (14) calendar days.  
(b) Postgraduate M.Tech., M.Pharm., MBA, and MCA candidates may borrow up to six (6) volumes for a period not exceeding twenty-one (21) calendar days.  
(c) Registered Ph.D. scholars are granted borrowing privileges for up to eight (8) text volumes for an extended period of thirty (30) calendar days.  
12.3. **Overdue Levies and Loss Liabilities**:  
(a) Any circulating book retained past the stipulated due date incurs an automated overdue fine of ₹5 per volume per day for the initial seven overdue days, escalating to ₹10 per volume per day for all subsequent days until the book is restored to the circulation desk.  
(b) Borrowers who misplace, mutilate, deface, or lose an issued library book are strictly obligated to either provide an exact replacement copy of the identical or subsequent edition published within the preceding two years or remit double the current market purchase price of the volume plus an administrative processing charge of ₹250.  
(c) Degree completion clearances, final transcripts, and migration certificates will be withheld until all library books and accrued overdue penalties are fully discharged.  
12.4. **Special Book Bank Scheme for Underrepresented Categories**:  
(a) The Central Library administers a specialized Government Book Bank Scheme catering exclusively to students belonging to Scheduled Caste (SC), Scheduled Tribe (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC-NCL) whose familial annual income falls within statutory state ceilings.  
(b) Eligible candidates receive a complete curated kit of foundational semester textbooks covering all enrolled core subjects for the entire duration of the instructional semester upon paying a nominal caution fee.  
(c) All book bank text volumes must be returned in usable condition within five (5) working days following the conclusion of the final subject End-Semester Examination.

### Section 13: Laboratory, Workshop Safety, and Research Protocol Standards

13.1. **Mandatory Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**:  
(a) Entry into mechanical machine workshops, carpentry shops, welding facilities, foundry laboratories, chemical processing units, and high-voltage electrical engineering laboratories requires strict adherence to mandatory PPE guidelines.  
(b) Every student working in mechanical workshops must wear heavy-duty industrial cotton work overalls, non-conductive steel-toed protective safety boots, and impact-resistant safety goggles during all active operational cycles. Loose clothing, wrist jewelry, rings, long unsecured hair, and open sandals or slippers are strictly prohibited in all workshop areas.  
(c) In chemical engineering, environmental science, and pharmacy wet laboratories, students must wear clean white full-length cotton laboratory aprons, chemical-resistant nitrile gloves, and eye-protective splash shields when handling volatile reagents, corrosive acids, or biological samples.  
13.2. **Equipment Operation and Breakage Assessment**:  
(a) No student is permitted to energize, operate, calibrate, or configure sophisticated analytical equipment, computerized numerical control (CNC) machine tools, tensile test rigs, or high-power transformers without the direct physical presence and supervision of the assigned laboratory technician or faculty course instructor.  
(b) Any accidental or negligent mechanical damage, electronic burn-out, or glassware breakage resulting from willful non-compliance with established standard operating procedures (SOPs) shall be evaluated by a three-member departmental breakage tribunal.  
(c) The assessed equipment repair or replacement expenditure shall be charged directly against the student's caution money deposit, and repeat infractions shall lead to immediate debarment from practical examinations.  
13.3. **Hazardous Material Protocols and Waste Disposal**:  
(a) Toxic organic solvents, halogenated compounds, concentrated mineral acids, and bio-hazardous microbiological cultures must be discarded exclusively into designated color-coded chemical waste collection drums positioned in departmental fume hoods.  
(b) Pouring unneutralized hazardous chemicals directly into public sinks, sewer lines, or open storm-water drains is an environmental offense subject to a mandatory disciplinary levy of ₹10,000 and mandatory disciplinary community service.

### Section 14: Career Training, Industrial Internships, and Campus Placement Regulations

14.1. **Training and Placement Cell (T&P) Jurisdiction**:  
The centralized Training and Placement Cell of the Institute coordinates all campus recruitment drives, pre-placement talks, corporate industrial internships, and soft-skills finishing programs for enrolled final-year and pre-final-year students.  
14.2. **Mandatory Industrial Internship Requirement**:  
(a) Every undergraduate B.Tech. student must successfully undergo a mandatory minimum of eight (8) weeks of verified practical industrial training or corporate internship during the summer vacations following the 4th semester or 6th semester.  
(b) The internship host enterprise or public-sector undertaking (PSU) must be vetted and formally endorsed by the Head of Department and the Institute Training & Placement Officer prior to commencement.  
(c) At the beginning of the 7th semester, the candidate must tender an official corporate internship completion certificate, a comprehensive technical training logbook, and undergo a formal oral evaluation before a departmental faculty committee carrying 2 mandatory curriculum credits.  
14.3. **One Student, One Job Policy and Exceptions**:  
(a) To ensure equitable career opportunities across the entire student population, the Institute enforces a strict **"One Student, One Job"** policy during campus recruitment drives. Once a student receives a verified formal employment offer letter from any recruiting organization, they are automatically de-registered from participating in subsequent campus interviews.  
(b) **Dream and Super-Dream Exceptions**:  
A student holding a standard job offer (annual Cost to Company below ₹8.00 Lakhs per annum) may register for a subsequent "Dream Company" drive offering an annual compensation package exceeding ₹12.00 Lakhs per annum. A candidate holding a Dream offer is permitted one final opportunity to participate in a "Super-Dream Company" recruitment process where the total annual remuneration package exceeds ₹20.00 Lakhs per annum.  
(c) Any candidate who accepts an employment offer and subsequently absents themselves from the joining date without prior recorded consent from the T&P Cell shall be permanently blacklisted from all institutional alumni placement networks.

### Section 15: Student Extracurricular Activities, Clubs, and Professional Societies

15.1. **Chartered Student Bodies**:  
The Institute officially recognizes student branches of international and national professional engineering organizations, including the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), Computer Society of India (CSI), American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME), Indian Society for Technical Education (ISTE), and the Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE-BAJA).  
15.2. **Institutional Financial Sanctions for Competitions**:  
(a) Student design teams participating in recognized national and international engineering competitions (such as SAE BAJA, Formula Student India, Smart India Hackathon, and University Rover Challenge) may apply for matching technical grants from the Institute Technical Development Fund.  
(b) All technical grant proposals must be countersigned by a permanent faculty advisor and approved by the Dean of Student Welfare.  
(c) Receipts, purchase logs, chassis telemetry data, and physical inventory must be submitted to the Institute Finance Section within fifteen (15) days of returning from the competition venue.

16.1. The Academic Council of SGSITS, as the supreme academic authority of the autonomous institute, reserves the inherent prerogative to amend, modify, augment, or repeal any provision of these regulations at any regular or extraordinary meeting.  
16.2. In emergency situations or unrepresented extenuating contingencies not explicitly contemplated by these ordinances, the Director, in consultation with the Chairman of the Academic Council, is authorized to issue administrative decrees to ensure equity, academic continuity, and institutional order.
